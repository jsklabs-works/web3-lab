Glossary

Every word used in the course, in plain language, in the order the lessons introduce them.

Lesson 1: Why blockchain

Money system
Any way people keep track of who owns money and who paid whom, for example a bank.
Ledger
A record book listing every payment: who paid whom, and how much.
Transaction
One entry in the ledger. Usually a payment, like "A pays B 50".
Node
A computer that keeps its own full copy of the ledger and checks every new entry.
Network
All the nodes, connected to each other over the internet.
Consensus
The nodes agreeing on one single version of the ledger.
Blockchain
A ledger copied across many nodes, stored as pages (called blocks) linked together. Lessons 2 to 5 explain how.

Lesson 2: Hashing

Data
Any information: text, numbers, a file, a whole ledger.
Hash function
A fixed recipe that turns any data into a short code. The same data always gives the same code.
Hash
The short code a hash function produces. It works like a fingerprint of the data.
SHA-256
The hash function Bitcoin uses. Its hash is always 64 characters long.
Hexadecimal
The characters 0 to 9 and a to f, used to write hashes and addresses.

Lesson 3: Blocks and the chain

Block
One page of the ledger: a group of transactions plus a little extra information.
Chain
Blocks kept in order, each one pointing back to the block before it.
Prev hash
The previous block's hash, written inside each block. It is the link of the chain.
Genesis block
The very first block. It has no block before it.
Tampering
Secretly changing data that is already recorded.

Lesson 4: Wallets, keys and signatures

Key
A very large number used to sign or check messages.
Private key
Your secret key. It creates signatures. Never share it.
Public key
The key made from your private key. It lets anyone check your signatures. Safe to share.
Address
A short version (a hash) of your public key. Like an account number that others use to pay you.
Signature
A code made from a message plus your private key. It proves the message came from you and wasn't changed.
Verify
Check a signature using the public key.
Wallet
An app that keeps your keys and signs for you. Your coins are recorded on the blockchain, not inside the app.
Seed phrase
12 or 24 words that back up your private keys. Whoever has them controls your money.

Lesson 5: Mining

Mining
Creating the next block by solving a guessing puzzle, then adding it to the end of the chain.
Miner
A computer, or the person running it, that does mining.
Nonce (mining)
A number the miner keeps changing until the block's hash starts with enough zeros.
Difficulty
How hard the puzzle is, for example how many zeros the hash must start with.
Proof of Work
The rule that you may add a block only after showing you did the puzzle work. Bitcoin uses it.
Reward
What the block creator earns: newly created coins plus the fees from the block's transactions.
51% attack
Trying to rewrite history by controlling more than half of the network's power.
Proof of Stake
Another way to choose who adds blocks: people lock up coins as a deposit and get picked at random. Ethereum uses it.
Validator
Someone who has locked up coins and takes turns adding blocks in Proof of Stake.
Stake
Coins locked up as a deposit. Cheaters lose part of it.

Recap: the big picture

Bitcoin (BTC)
The first blockchain, made for digital money. BTC is its coin.
Ethereum (ETH)
A blockchain that can also run programs (Lesson 7). ETH is its coin.
Coin
The built-in money of a blockchain, used to pay and to pay fees: BTC, ETH, SOL.
Token
Money or points created by a program on someone else's blockchain, like USDC on Ethereum.
Exchange
A company or app where you buy and sell coins with normal money.
Stablecoin
A token designed to always be worth $1, backed by real dollars held in reserve.
Volatile
Price goes up and down a lot, quickly.
Layer 2
A faster, cheaper network built on top of Ethereum that saves its results back to Ethereum.

Lesson 6: The life of a transaction

ETH (ether)
Ethereum's coin. You send it, and you pay fees with it.
Test network (testnet)
A practice copy of a blockchain whose coins are free and worthless.
Sepolia
Ethereum's main test network.
Fee (gas)
A small charge for a transaction, paid to whoever adds it to a block.
Gwei
A tiny unit of ETH used for fees: 1 gwei = 0.000000001 ETH.
Mempool
The waiting room where signed transactions sit until they're put in a block.
Block explorer
A website for looking up anything on a blockchain. Etherscan is the one for Ethereum.
Transaction hash
A transaction's fingerprint. You use it to look the transaction up.
Nonce (account)
Counts how many transactions an account has sent: 0, 1, 2… Different from the mining nonce.
Confirmation
Each new block added after yours. More confirmations mean it's harder to undo.

Lesson 7: Smart contracts

Program (code)
Step-by-step instructions that a computer follows exactly.
Smart contract
A program stored on the blockchain. Anyone can use it, and nobody can change it.
Solidity
The programming language most Ethereum smart contracts are written in.
Deploy
Put a contract on the blockchain. After that its code can't be changed.
Contract address
Where a deployed contract lives, like a wallet address. It can hold ETH.
Function
One action a contract offers, like deposit() or withdraw().
Call
Use a function, for example "call withdraw()".
State
The information a contract remembers, like who its owner is and how much ETH it holds.
Owner
In our example, the account that deployed the contract. The contract's rules give it special rights.
Revert
A rule failed, so the whole call is cancelled and nothing changes.

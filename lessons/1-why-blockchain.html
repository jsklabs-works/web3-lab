Lesson 1: Why do we need blockchain?

A blockchain is a record book that thousands of strangers keep copies of, with rules that make them all agree on the same version.

Forget computers for a minute. Every money system is really a record book (ledger) of who owns what and who paid whom. The big question is: who holds the book?

Today's way: one boss holds the book

When Alice pays Bob ₹100, nothing physical moves. She asks the bank to update its book: Alice −100, Bob +100. Everyone has to trust that book.

  • The bank can make a mistake or change an entry, and you can't check its book.
  • The bank can say no: freeze your account or block a payment.
  • One place can fail: if its server goes down or is hacked, everyone is stuck.
  • It sits in the middle of every payment and charges a fee for it.

The blockchain way: everyone holds a copy

There's no bank. The payment is announced to everyone, and each person writes it into their own copy. The computers keeping copies are called nodes; Bitcoin has thousands of them around the world.

  • Nobody can secretly change history. One changed copy won't match the others, so it gets rejected.
  • Nobody can block you just because they don't like you.
  • No single point of failure. If some nodes go offline, the others still have the book.
  • Anyone can check. The book is public, so you can verify instead of trusting.

The key rule

Only what was announced and agreed by the network is accepted. This is called consensus. To fake a change, a hacker would have to change more than half of all the copies at the same moment, which is practically impossible. That's called a 51% attack.

Try it (pen and paper, about 5 minutes)

  1. Three friends each keep a notebook. Every time someone pays someone, all three write it down.
  2. Write 4 entries yourself, like "A pays B ₹50".
  3. Now pretend B secretly changes their notebook to "A pays B ₹500".
  4. How would the group notice, and whose version would they believe?

Check yourself

In the bank picture, what happens if a hacker changes your balance on the bank's computer? In the shared picture, what would the same hacker need to do?

Show answer

Bank: the bank's book is the only truth, so the wrong balance is simply believed, and you have no copy to prove otherwise.

Shared: one changed copy doesn't match the others, so it's rejected. The hacker would need to change more than half of all copies at once.

2. How this works in real life

Key words
Bitcoin (BTC)
The first blockchain, made for digital money. BTC is its coin.
Ethereum (ETH)
A blockchain that can also run programs (Lesson 7). ETH is its coin.
Exchange
A company or app where you buy and sell coins with normal money.
In the lessonsIn the real world
WalletApps like MetaMask, or hardware wallets (USB devices that keep your private key offline)
NodesAbout 20,000 computers worldwide for Bitcoin. Anyone can run one.
MinerWarehouses of special machines (ASICs) for Bitcoin; validators for Ethereum
New blockBitcoin about every 10 minutes; Ethereum about every 12 seconds
Getting coinsMost people buy on an exchange app. Exchanges usually hold your keys for you, which is like a bank again.

3. Why "cryptocurrency," and where does the value come from?

Crypto = cryptography, the maths of hashing and signatures that secures it. It doesn't mean secret; every transaction is public. Currency = it's used as money.

Nobody guarantees its value. It's worth what people will pay for it, like gold:

GoldBitcoin
Rare: limited amount on EarthRare: only 21 million will ever exist; it's in the code
Costly to dig outCostly to mine (Lesson 5)
Hard to fakeImpossible to fake: signatures, and no double spending
Valuable because people agree it isSame

Compare the rupee, which is backed by the government and the RBI. Bitcoin has nobody backing it, so its price is purely supply and demand.

4. Why are some cryptocurrencies unstable?

Key words
Volatile
Price goes up and down a lot, quickly.
Stablecoin
A token designed to always be worth $1, backed by real dollars held in reserve.
  • News and sentiment: one tweet, ban or approval can move prices 10–20% in a day.
  • Speculation: many buyers are betting on the price, not using the coin.
  • Small market: a few big holders can push the price around.
  • Always open: trading runs 24/7, with no pause.

Exception, stablecoins (USDC, USDT): designed to always equal $1, backed by real dollars held in reserve.

Not financial advice: many coins have gone to zero. Learn on test networks, where coins are free, and never put in money you can't afford to lose.

5. Different blockchains for different jobs

Key words
Coin
The built-in money of a blockchain, used to pay and to pay fees: BTC, ETH, SOL.
Token
Money or points created by a program on someone else's blockchain, like USDC on Ethereum.
Layer 2
A faster, cheaper network built on top of Ethereum that saves its results back to Ethereum.
BlockchainBest atTrade-off
BitcoinSimple, very secure digital moneySlow; payments only
EthereumSmart contracts: programs on the chain (Lesson 7)Fees can be high when busy
SolanaVery fast and cheapFewer nodes, more centralized
Layer 2sMake Ethereum cheaper by bundling transactionsNewer, more moving parts
Private chainsCompanies tracking supply chainsInvite-only, not really decentralized

A coin is native to its own blockchain (BTC, ETH, SOL). A token is created by a smart contract on someone else's chain (USDC, and your LRN on Ethereum).

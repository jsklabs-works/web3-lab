Lesson 4: Wallets, keys and signatures

A wallet is a pair of keys. The private key signs, the public key lets anyone check the signature, and the address is what you share.

The problem

On a shared ledger anyone can announce a transaction. What stops Bob from shouting "A pays B 500" and pretending it came from A? We need a way for A to prove "I really said this" that anyone can check, but nobody can fake.

The solution: a pair of keys

Key words
Key
A very large number used to sign or check messages.
Private key
Your secret key. It creates signatures. Never share it.
Public key
The key made from your private key. It lets anyone check your signatures. Safe to share.
Address
A short version (a hash) of your public key. Like an account number that others use to pay you.
Wallet
An app that keeps your keys and signs for you. Your coins are recorded on the blockchain, not inside the app.
Seed phrase
12 or 24 words that back up your private keys. Whoever has them controls your money.

Each arrow works in one direction only, like hashing. From the private key anyone can calculate the public key, but never the other way round. The address is a shortened hash of the public key: what you share to receive money, like an account number.

Analogy: the private key is your personal rubber stamp, which only you hold. The public key is a photo of the stamp's print that you show everyone. People can compare a stamped paper with the photo, but nobody can make your stamp from a photo.

⚠️ Whoever has your private key owns your money. There's no "forgot password" and no bank to call. The 12-word seed phrase that wallets show you is a backup of your private key. Never share it with anyone. Real support will never ask for it.

Signing and checking a transaction

Key words
Signature
A code made from a message plus your private key. It proves the message came from you and wasn't changed.
Verify
Check a signature using the public key.

A signature is made from both the message and the private key. So:

  • Change the message, even one character, and the signature no longer fits → invalid.
  • Sign with a different key (Bob pretending to be Alice) and it doesn't match Alice's public key → invalid.
  • Checking needs only the public key, so the whole network can verify without ever seeing Alice's secret.

The whole system rests on one thing: keeping the private key secret.

Check yourself

A stranger messages you: "I'm from MetaMask support. Send me your 12-word seed phrase so we can fix your wallet." What happens if you do it?

Show answer

Never share it. With your seed phrase they can rebuild your private key, sign as you, and take everything, and nobody can undo it. Anyone asking for it is a scammer, every time.
